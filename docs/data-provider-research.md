# EQUINOX data-provider research — 2026-10-02

This is a documentation review, not a live-data verification or a legal opinion. No market-data payload, subscription entitlement, CORS behavior, or provider availability was tested. Among the reviewed options, there is no verified, entirely public/keyless pipeline that meets all of EQUINOX's synchronization, adjustment, history, and usage-rights requirements. Do not present that as proof that no such provider exists.

The calendar must come from the regular U.S. cash-equity session, including [NYSE holiday/early-close schedules](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars) and the [Nasdaq schedule](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stock-market-holiday-schedule). Regular close is 16:00 Eastern; designated early closes are 13:00 Eastern. Convert each actual session close through `America/New_York`, not a fixed UTC offset. Keep a versioned calendar with extraordinary closures; weekdays alone are insufficient. The parent app should bound its supported calendar range rather than extrapolate unverified schedules.

Use the intended instruments: [GOOGL is Alphabet Class A](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/googl); [ISRG trades on Nasdaq](https://isrg.intuitive.com/); [TSM is TSMC's NYSE-listed ADS](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/faq), distinct from Taiwan-listed `2330`. Crypto must be USD spot pairs for BTC and ETH from a declared venue, not a stablecoin pair, derivative, or substitute index.

Recommended v1 contract and gates:

1. Accept manual current USD prices, quantities, and DKK-per-USD FX with explicit as-of timestamps and a visible manual status. Do not infer that they are current merely because the user entered them. Keep current valuation inputs distinct from adjusted historical return data.
2. A historical import needs instrument identifiers, venue, quote currency, source and acquisition time, session dates, scheduled close timestamps, observation/bucket timestamps, price basis, dividend treatment, FX units and timing, and any missing/stale observations. Preserve raw files and transformation version for reproducibility. A hash can identify bytes; it cannot establish authenticity.
3. A normalized file's `splitAdjusted: true`, timestamp labels, source URL, or user declaration cannot prove its semantics. Structural validation may establish only that the file is well formed. Provider adjustment documentation plus a validated raw-provider adapter, or an independently reviewed source dataset and reproducible transformation, is needed before calling the data verified. Otherwise retain an unverified status and withhold any result that requires verified data.
4. Choose and disclose one stock-return basis: split-adjusted price returns excluding cash dividends, or split-and-dividend-adjusted returns. Do not mix the two across equities. A provider's cash-dividend adjustment is not a promise of the user's after-tax total return; ADR fees, withholding, and personal cash handling need separate treatment.
5. For each expected session close, require all five asset observations and DKK-per-USD FX under the declared timing policy. For a one-minute crypto close convention, require the exact bucket ending at that close and preserve its interval; do not call the bucket-end timestamp the trade timestamp. If a literal observation at the exact instant is required, candle data alone is insufficient. Missing bars remain missing.
6. Build returns only between consecutive expected U.S. sessions with complete aligned endpoints. Never join two dates across a missing session and call the result a daily return; never forward-fill equity/crypto prices or FX to satisfy coverage. With USD historical level `P` and DKK per USD `F`, simple DKK return is `(P[t] / P[t-1]) * (F[t] / F[t-1]) - 1`.
7. Require at least 252 valid common one-session returns, which requires at least 253 price snapshots if there are no gaps. Also require at least 95% coverage of the expected return intervals in a fixed, disclosed window ending at the latest completed session. Report both counts, denominator, dates, and missing sessions. A file-supplied count or a denominator based only on its own rows is not coverage verification. Do not extend arbitrarily far backward until the threshold happens to pass.
8. Enforce a declared latest-observation age independently of historical completeness. Old data can have perfect historical coverage. Crypto weekend moves are included between Friday and Monday equity-close snapshots; annualizing this session-sampled risk uses a declared trading-session convention.

A purely client-side application cannot conceal a vendor secret embedded in its JavaScript or network requests. Do not ask for a secret simply to unlock a demo. First use manual inputs/imports with honest provenance. If a verified live/history provider later requires a secret, the concrete next step is a server-side adapter/proxy with server-held credentials and the required data rights; neither a public CORS relay nor a browser environment variable resolves those constraints. Until that path is verified, risk should explain which evidence is missing rather than show fabricated observations or a reassuring quality badge.
